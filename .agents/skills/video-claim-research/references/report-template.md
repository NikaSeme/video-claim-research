# Report Template

Use this structure for concise PDFs.

## Sections

1. Title, source video, and date analyzed.
2. Bottom line in 3-5 sentences.
3. What the video claims.
4. Source attribution and scope of verification.
5. Evidence for the claim.
6. Evidence against or alternative explanations.
7. Source quality and gaps.
8. Likelihood, confidence, and opinion.
9. Questions for the user.
10. Short source list.

## Ratings

Use plain-language ratings:

- Source quality: strong, decent, mixed, weak, unknown.
- Claim likelihood: very likely, likely, plausible but unproven, unlikely, very unlikely, unclear.
- Evidence independence: distinguish vendor statements, independent research, and direct tests. Never rate the person.
- Confidence: high, medium, low.

Explain each rating in one or two sentences.

## Visuals

Use a small number of useful visuals:

- original explanatory diagrams, clearly labelled as illustrations rather than captured evidence;
- simple timeline;
- claim-to-evidence table;
- source-quality table.

Do not use decorative visuals if they reduce clarity. Source-video imagery is forbidden in deliverables, including screenshots, crops, thumbnails, clips, traces, and generated recreations. Follow `publication-policy.md` and record the final publication review.
